# Competitive standard: connected photography workflow

Verification date: **2026-09-04**. Public official product pages and vendor documentation only. Source update dates appear below when supplied; undated pages were checked on the verification date. No accounts were created, providers contacted, software installed, payments made, or authenticated workflows tested for this research.

“Documented” means the vendor describes a capability. It does not establish our independent measurement of quality, reliability, security, speed, or revenue impact. A capability absent from these pages is **unverified**, not proven absent. The proposed evaluation gates are our product requirements, not statements that LensLabs already passes them.

## Product conclusion

Do not position LensLabs as the first photography all-in-one. Pixieset already bundles business and gallery products and now documents connected culling, editing, and publication. Its editor also supports assisted selection and human correction. That directly overlaps the proposed platform. [Pixieset Suite](https://pixieset.com/pricing-suite/), [Photo Editor](https://pixieset.com/photo-editor/), [culling guide, June 19, 2026](https://website-help.pixieset.com/en/articles/15570404-culling-photos-in-pixieset-photo-editor).

Our **hypothesis to validate** is a better high-volume sports workflow: fast first delivery, reliable source identities across cameras, coverage of required athletes and moments, reversible human choices, understandable editing proposals, and exact client/version handoffs. Neither all-in-one branding nor a natural-language command box proves differentiation. Compare complete assignments and correction time, not feature counts or an invented generic accuracy score.

## Important current integration caveats

- **Pixieset:** its May 15 getting-started guide says publishing sends all photos in the project and does not allow choosing specific images at publication. Later culling/editing guides describe moving picks into Editing. Confirm the exact publication set in a controlled current-version test before using this as a competitive gap. The guide lists Chrome or the desktop app and recommends Apple M1 / RTX 2060-class hardware. [Getting started, May 15, 2026](https://website-help.pixieset.com/en/articles/14063290-getting-started-with-pixieset-photo-editor).
- **Capture One:** the quick-start guide says comments depend on the online collection and disappear on expiry/termination; its overview separately extends retention for ratings/tags in newer versions. These are different data types, not a blanket long-term-history promise. Preserve exact feedback versions in our own acceptance test. [Quick start](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/4411313408913-How-do-I-share-a-selection-of-photos), [overview](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403611619985-Capture-One-Live-overview).
- **PhotoDay:** private gallery lookup can use photographer-configured imported subject fields instead of individual access codes; the published article labels this beta. This documents an access mechanism, not independent security validation. [Private Gallery Lookup, August 12, 2024](https://support.photoday.io/en/articles/9339805-private-gallery-lookup).

## Price evidence relevant to consolidation

These are displayed public dollar prices, not account-specific quotes or a recommendation to cancel anything. Recheck currency, taxes, region, current checkout terms and existing annual obligations before a purchasing decision.

- **Pixieset Suite:** Starter displays $28/month billed annually or $35 monthly, with 100 GB photo storage; Pro $38/$50 with 2,000 GB; Ultimate $55/$65 with an “unlimited” photo-storage headline but a 1,000 GB RAW-upload footnote. Photo Editor is listed separately. Paid store plans advertise no platform sales commission; processing and fulfillment costs must still be assessed separately. [Suite pricing](https://pixieset.com/pricing-suite/).
- **Pixieset Editor:** displays $12/month billed annually or $18 monthly, with unlimited culling/editing and no per-image charges advertised. That statement does not make Suite storage or all external costs unlimited. [Editor pricing](https://pixieset.com/photo-editor/).
- **PhotoDay:** advertises no subscription, a 10% order transaction fee, 2.9% + $0.30 processing, and separately deducted lab costs. Its pricing page describes the 10% base as pre-tax/pre-shipping subtotal, while public terms describe a broader Order Value and a different example. **Do not calculate definitive effective fees until that discrepancy is resolved against the applicable agreement.** [Pricing](https://photoday.com/pricing), [terms](https://photoday.com/terms).
- **Pic-Time:** the automation page says sales campaigns are available on its permanent free plan; paid plans change branding, catalogs and sales-commission options. “Automation included” does not mean zero transaction or fulfillment costs. [Automation FAQ](https://www.pic-time.com/features/automation).

Other exact prices were intentionally not sampled: the first question is whether the photographer can migrate the relevant job successfully. A paid integration remains a cost, and a marketplace fee is not a canceled subscription. Record each pilot photographer’s actual starting bill and completed replacement acceptance tests.

## Next evidence to collect

1. Run the same permitted, representative sports/event assignment through incumbent workflows and LensLabs. Include two cameras, genuine varied photographs, motion blur, obscured subjects, meaningful closed eyes and a first-delivery deadline.
2. Record versions, hardware, image formats/dimensions, cache/network conditions, time to first useful preview, completed review, corrections, missed valuable frames, export and delivery.
3. Compare the client journey on a phone: invitation/access, exact-image feedback, revision, final download and the signed/payment state where supported.
4. Test restoration and migration with originals, metadata, edit versions and client decisions—not only rendered JPEGs or a gallery URL.
5. Treat sports entry-market fit, willingness to switch and time saved as unproven until real photographers complete these tests. Do not infer universal superiority from vendor demos or our synthetic import timings.
